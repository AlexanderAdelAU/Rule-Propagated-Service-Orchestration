What if?

A remote payment service

Online store→Internet→Payment service

Useful, but hard to test in context: how does it behave under real traffic, bursts, declines, errors and slow answers? And what does that do to the rest of the checkout?

Drop it into RPSO

One generator. One place.
Your service, unchanged.

PAYMENTS→T_in→Payment (REST)→Paid / Declined

The place calls the payment service's own endpoint, here a mock on Vercel. RPSO supplies the traffic, records every call from the caller's side, and replays the run on the model.

Watch it run

Press Play. Steady payments, then a burst of twelve: the queue builds at T_in, declines take their own route, failed calls stop at the place. Open it on its own page

What one run told us

30 payments, measured from the caller's side

  • 24 paid, 3 declined, each routed on the service's own answer
  • 3 failed at the place, with the reason: two gateway errors (HTTP 503), one answer slower than 2.5 s
  • The burst queued for up to 10.8 s; steady traffic hardly queued

Recorded on an ordinary desktop calling the service across the internet. A recorded run, not a simulation

And when the service is shared?

In-store payments go first

In-store payments and online checkout call the same gateway. During the online burst, the place serves the lower version first, so the customer at the till is not stuck behind the queue.
